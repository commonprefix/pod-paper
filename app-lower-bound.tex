\section{Lower bound on the number of replicas}\label{app:lower-bound}

\Cref{thm:pod-security} requires $n \geq 5\beta + 3\gamma + 1$ replicas. We
show that the term $5\beta$ is necessary for the properties that \podtm-core
achieves. The bound does not rely on the architecture of \podtm-core: replicas
may exchange messages with each other, but clients exchange messages only with
replicas.

\dotparagraph{Executions}
Let $\beta \geq 1$ and $n \leq 5\beta$, and consider a protocol with $n$
replicas, \beta of which are Byzantine. We partition the replicas into sets
$G_1, \ldots, G_5$ of at most \beta replicas each. We consider a writer
$\client_0$ and readers $\client_1, \client_2, \client_3$. Let \parTemp be a
timeliness parameter, which may be a function of $\delta$. Let $\theta_1$ be
its value for $\delta = 1$, and let $\lambda = \theta_1 + 2$. We define
executions $E_1, \ldots, E_5$, shown in \Cref{tab:lower-bound-executions}, in
which all parties use the same keys and random coins.

In every execution, all parties follow the protocol up to round \round, and
every message sent up to round \round has delay $1$. The only exception are the
messages of $\client_0$ in round \round. In $E_3$ client $\client_0$ is
malicious and sends no message from round \round on. In the other executions it
calls \opwrite{\tx}, and its messages have the delays of
\Cref{tab:lower-bound-executions}. We call them the \emph{write messages}.  All
other clients are honest in all executions.

A message sent after round \round has delay $1$ in $E_1$ and delay $\lambda$ in
the other executions. In $E_4$ and $E_5$ there are two exceptions, for messages
sent after round \round to a reader $\client_i$. A message from the set that is
Byzantine in $E_i$ has delay $3\lambda$. Any other message to $\client_1$ has
delay $1$, as in $E_1$.

In $E_i$, for $i \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, the Byzantine replicas follow the protocol,
but send nothing to $\client_i$ after round \round. After round \round, the
Byzantine replicas of $E_4$ and $E_5$ send to each $\client_i$ the messages that
they send to $\client_i$ in $E_i$, and nothing else. Finally, $E_3$ does not
involve \tx. We choose \tx as a transaction that $\client_3$ does not output in
$E_3$ at round $\round + 2\lambda - 1$.

\Cref{lem:lower-bound-replicas} shows that an honest replica sends the same
messages in two executions until the write messages, or a message sent after
round \round, reach it. \Cref{lem:lower-bound-readers} uses this to show that
the readers produce in $E_4$ and $E_5$ the same outputs as in $E_1, E_2, E_3$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lower-bound-replicas}
    Let $R$ be a replica that is honest in two of the executions, and let $s
    \leq \round + \lambda$. If one of the two executions is $E_1$, let $s \leq
    \round + 1$. Assume that $R$ receives the write messages in the same round
    in both executions, or after round $s$ or never in both. Then $R$ sends the
    same messages up to round $s$ in both executions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    All parties follow the protocol up to round \round, with the same coins, and
    messages sent up to round \round have delay $1$. By induction on the round,
    apart from the write messages, every party receives the same messages up to
    round $\round + 1$ in all executions. A message sent to a replica after
    round \round is received after round $\round + 1$ in $E_1$, and after round
    $\round + \lambda$ in the other executions. Hence $R$ receives the same
    messages up to round $s$ in both executions.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lower-bound-readers}
    Assume that the protocol is a \parTemp-timely secure \podtm with
    confirmation within $\parConf = 2\delta$ and past-perfection within
    $\parPerf = \delta$. Then the following outputs are valid.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item In $E_1$ and in $E_4$, client $\client_1$ outputs the same
            $(\Pod_1, \cert_1)$ at round $\round + 2$, where \tx appears in
            $\Pod_1.\txSet$ with $\rmax^1 \leq \round + \theta_1$.
        \item In $E_2$, $E_4$ and $E_5$, client $\client_2$ outputs the same
            $(\Pod_2, \cert_2)$ at round $\round + 2\lambda$, where \tx appears in
            $\Pod_2.\txSet$ with $\rconf^2 \neq \bot$.
        \item In $E_3$ and in $E_5$, client $\client_3$ outputs the same
            $(\Pod_3, \cert_3)$ at round $\round + 2\lambda - 1$, where \tx does
            not appear in $\Pod_3.\txSet$ and $\Pod_3.\rperf \geq \round +
            \theta_1 + 1$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    The three outputs are valid by completeness. The output of a reader
    $\client_i$ is determined by the messages that it receives up to the round
    of the output. We derive the output in $E_i$, and show that $\client_i$
    receives the same messages in the same rounds in $E_4$ or $E_5$. By
    definition of $E_4$ and $E_5$, these messages have the same delays, and the
    Byzantine replicas send the same messages as in $E_i$. The replicas that
    are Byzantine in $E_i$ send nothing to $\client_i$ in $E_i$, and their
    messages have delay $3\lambda$ in $E_4$ and $E_5$, so they arrive after the
    output. It remains to show that the other honest replicas send the same
    messages.

    \emph{Item 1.}
    In $E_1$ we have $\delta = 1$, and the honest $\client_0$ writes \tx at
    round \round. Confirmation within $\parConf = 2\delta$ gives that \tx
    appears in $\Pod_1.\txSet$, and \parTemp-timeliness gives $\rmax^1 \leq
    \round + \theta_1$. The messages that $\client_1$ receives up to round
    $\round + 2$ are sent up to round $\round + 1$ by replicas in $G_1, G_2,
    G_3, G_5$. In $E_4$, the replicas in $G_1, G_2, G_5$ are honest and receive
    the write messages at round $\round + 1$, as in $E_1$. By
    \Cref{lem:lower-bound-replicas}, they send the same messages up to round
    $\round + 1$.

    \emph{Item 2.}
    In $E_2$ we have $\delta = \lambda$, and the honest $\client_0$ writes \tx
    at round \round. Confirmation within $\parConf = 2\delta$ gives that \tx
    appears in $\Pod_2.\txSet$ with $\rconf^2 \neq \bot$. The messages that
    $\client_2$ receives up to round $\round + 2\lambda$ are sent up to round
    $\round + \lambda$ by replicas in $G_1, \ldots, G_4$. Among them, the
    honest replicas of $E_4$ are $G_1, G_2, G_4$ and those of $E_5$ are $G_2,
    G_3, G_4$. They receive the write messages at the same round as in $E_2$.
    By \Cref{lem:lower-bound-replicas}, they send the same messages up to round
    $\round + \lambda$.

    \emph{Item 3.}
    In $E_3$ we have $\delta = \lambda$. Past-perfection within $\parPerf =
    \delta$ gives $\Pod_3.\rperf \geq \round + \lambda - 1 = \round + \theta_1 +
    1$, and \tx does not appear in $\Pod_3.\txSet$ by the choice of \tx. The
    messages that $\client_3$ receives up to round $\round + 2\lambda - 1$ are
    sent up to round $\round + \lambda - 1$ by replicas in $G_1, G_3, G_4, G_5$.
    In $E_5$, the replicas in $G_3, G_4, G_5$ are honest and receive the write
    messages at round $\round + \lambda$. By \Cref{lem:lower-bound-replicas},
    they send the same messages up to round $\round + \lambda - 1$ as in $E_3$,
    where they never receive write messages.
\end{proof}

\dotparagraph{Intuition}
\Cref{tab:lower-bound-readers} summarizes \Cref{lem:lower-bound-readers}. For
each reader, it shows the round at which each set received \tx, according to the
messages that the reader receives, and the output of the reader. Each row
occurs in an execution where the four sets that the reader hears from are
honest, and the fifth set is Byzantine. These are the executions $E_1, E_2,
E_3$. A reader cannot tell whether a set is honest.

The rows of $\client_1$ and $\client_2$ differ only in $G_3$, apart from dashes.
Hence both rows occur in $E_4$, where $G_3$ is Byzantine. The confirmation
bounds property then requires $\rconf \leq \rmax \leq \round + \theta_1$. The
rows of $\client_2$ and $\client_3$ differ only in $G_1$, because $G_3, G_4$
receive \tx at round $\round + \lambda$ and $\client_3$ outputs based on
messages that they sent earlier. Hence both rows occur in $E_5$, where $G_1$ is
Byzantine. The past-perfection safety property then requires $\rconf \geq
\rperf > \round + \theta_1$.  No value of \rconf satisfies both requirements.

\begin{theorem}[Lower bound on the number of replicas]\label{thm:lower-bound}
    Let $\beta \geq 1$ and $n \leq 5\beta$, and let \parTemp be any function of
    the network delay $\delta$.  Consider the partially synchronous model with
    $n$ replicas, \beta of which are Byzantine.  There is no protocol that
    satisfies, except with negligible probability, the properties of a
    \parTemp-timely secure \podtm (\Cref{def:podsec,def:pod-timely}) with
    confirmation within $\parConf = 2\delta$ and past-perfection within
    $\parPerf = \delta$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    Assume, towards a contradiction, that such a protocol exists, and consider the executions $E_1, \ldots, E_5$ defined above.
    We use a constant number of properties of the protocol in five executions, so a negligible failure probability does not affect the argument.
    We read the safety properties of \Cref{def:podsec} as excluding any two valid pods of the stated form, including pods that honest clients output.
    The outputs of \Cref{lem:lower-bound-readers} satisfy $\rmax^1 \leq \round + \theta_1 < \Pod_3.\rperf$.

    \emph{Case 1: $\rconf^2 > \rmax^1$.}
    In $E_4$, clients $\client_1$ and $\client_2$ output the valid $(\Pod_1, \cert_1)$ and $(\Pod_2, \cert_2)$.
    Transaction \tx appears in both, with $\rmax^1 < \rconf^2$.
    This contradicts the confirmation bounds property.

    \emph{Case 2: $\rconf^2 \leq \rmax^1$.}
    In $E_5$, clients $\client_3$ and $\client_2$ output the valid $(\Pod_3, \cert_3)$ and $(\Pod_2, \cert_2)$.
    Transaction \tx does not appear in $\Pod_3.\txSet$, and it appears in $\Pod_2.\txSet$ with $\rconf^2 \neq \bot$ and $\rconf^2 < \Pod_3.\rperf$.
    This contradicts the past-perfection safety property.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
    For $\gamma = 0$ the bound is tight, since \podtm-core achieves these properties with $n = 5\beta + 1$.
    The proof relies on $\parPerf = \delta$ and on timeliness.
    We do not claim the bound for a larger \parPerf, or for a secure \podtm that is not timely.
\end{remark}
