\section{Pod with transaction propagation} \label{sec:pod-with-propagation}
In the construction of \Cref{sec:pod-core-const}, replicas do not communicate with each other.
In this section we introduce and additional property, \emph{Common subset after \parAgr}, and describe a construction that satisfies it.

\begin{definition}[Common subset after \parAgr]
    If an honest client observes transaction \tx as \confirmed,
    then every other honest client will observe \tx as \confirmed after at most \parAgr rounds.
    That is, for any honest clients $\client_1$, $\client_2$ and rounds $\round_1, \round_2 > \round_1 + \parAgr$,
    if $\tx \in \podView{\client_1}{\round_1}$ and $\tx.\rconf \neq \bot$, then $\tx \in \podView{\client_2}{\round_2}$ and $\tx.\rconf \neq \bot$.
\end{definition}
Observe that the \emph{confirmation agreement after \parAgr} property generalizes the \emph{confirmation monotonicity} property of \emph{pod core} (\Cref{sec:pod-model}) for views of two honest clients $\client_1$, $\client_2$.

\begin{construction}[Pod with transaction propagation]\label{cons:pod-with-prop}
    \oa{If we have time, describe in words (no pseudocode).}
    In order to satisfy the \emph{common subset} property,
    the construction requires a transaction propagation mechanism, that ensures that all honest replicas see the same set of transactions,
    even for transactions sent by malicious parties.
    We denote by \tProp the maximum number of rounds it takes for a transaction to be delivered by all replicas.
\end{construction}

\subsection{Security}
\begin{lemma}[Agreement after \parAgr]
    \Cref{cons:pod-with-prop} satisfies the \emph{agreement after \parAgr} property for $\parAgr = \tProp$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    \oa{Proof sketch: a malicious replica returns \tx only to the first client, making the difference
    between being confirmed and not. Using gossip/relaying, after \tProp all honest will have seen \tx, hence the second client also sees it as confirmed. }
\end{proof}
