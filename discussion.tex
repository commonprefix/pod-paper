\section{Discussion}

\oa{NEW section}

In this work we presented pod, a novel consensus layer that finalizes transactions with the optimal one-round-trip latency. Pod eliminates communication among replicas. Instead, clients read the state of the system by performing lightweight computations on the logs that replicas send to them. As no validator has a particular role in pod (as compared to leaders, block proposers, miners, etc. in similar protocols), pod achieves censorship resistance by default, without any extra mechanisms or additional cost. Furthermore, validator misbehavior, such as voting in incompatible ways or censoring confirmed transactions, is accountable.

We then presented an efficient and censorship-resistanct auction mechanism, leveraging pod as a bulletin board. We saw how the accountability, offered by pod, is also inherrited by applications built on it -- the auctioneer cannot censor confirmed bids without being detected.

On a technical remark, pod differs from standard notions of consensus because it does not offer an agreement property, neither to validators nor to clients. A client reading the pod obtains a past-perfect round \rperf, and it is guaranteed to have received all transactions that obtained a confirmed round \rconf such that $\rconf \leq \rperf$. It is also guaranteed to have received all transactions that can potentially obtain an $\rconf \leq \rperf$ in the future, even though the transaction presently appears to the client as unconfirmed. However, the client cannot tell which unconfirmed transactions will become confirmed, and, what is more, a transaction might appear confirmed to one client and unconfirmed to another (in this case, this will be transaction written by a malicious client).
